Welcome to the Targeted Drug Delivery Lab

Within our laboratory, we are actively involved in the advancement of novel, target-centric drug delivery platforms. Our principal focus revolves around elucidating specific molecular targets, with a particular emphasis on discerning distinctive ligands tailored specifically for neoplastic cells. Additionally, our endeavors extend to the exploration of the repercussions associated with genetic manipulation during the process of pancreatic tumorigenesis.

OUR RESEARCH PROJECTS

Means and Measures to Improve treatment Efficacy in Pancreatic Cancer

Aptamers are small oligonucleotide sequences that specifically bind to the cell of interest and carry therapeutic cargo into the cell. My focus lies on the potential of aptamers as a targeted therapeutic strategy. These small, customizable oligonucleotide sequences exhibit a unique capability to bind specifically to PDAC cells, offering a promising avenue for delivering therapeutic payloads. The compact size of aptamers is proving advantageous, particularly in navigating the dense microenvironment characteristic of PDAC. Their adaptability allows for precise customization, addressing the intricate molecular pathomechanism of PDAC. This investigation highlights the potential of aptamers as a effective and tailored tool in the realm of PDAC targeted treatment approaches.

Targeting Heterogeneity of Cancer associated Fibroblasts

The intricate interplay between tumor cells and the fibrotic stroma in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) have complex interactions. Despite the consensus on the obstructive role of the tumor stroma in PDAC treatments. This highlights the urgency of investigating the identifying specific molecular mechanisms, characterization, the regulation of cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) within the tumor microenvironment to uncover molecular intricacies, providing crucial insights that can reshape our approach to treating PDAC.

Role of Sphingolipid Rheostat in Pancreatic Cancer

Sphingolipids metabolism emerge as critical regulators in pancreatic cancer initiation, progression, and metastasis. Enzymes such as acid sphingomyelinase (SMPD1), acid ceramidase (ASAH1), and ceramide synthases (CerSs) intricately modulate the sphingolipid rheostat. Perturbations in their expression disrupt this balance, exerting significant influence on signaling pathways in PDAC. We are identifying the specific role of SMPD1, ASAH1, and CerSs in various PDAC models. Targeting these enzymes may offers promising insights for targeted therapeutic strategies in the context of PDAC

Mutli-omics Signature for Diagnostic and Treatment Stratification of PDAC

Leveraging multi-omics studies to refine diagnosis and treatment strategies, we are integrating genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics which allows for a comprehensive molecular profile, enabling the identification of precise biomarkers and molecular subtypes in pancreatic cancer. We identified various biomarker using human samples which may use in diagnosis of PDAC patients, this approach holds the potential to personalize treatment strategies and improve outcomes in PDAC.
